## Script Rulebook Shared With James

- James asked for the current video script-writing rules. The concise rulebook shared back was:
- One food per video, judged per `100g`.
- Use the locked 9-section shell: intro, fats, carbs, proteins, vitamins, minerals, pros, cons, outro.
- Use the 7-section scored body: fats, carbs, proteins, vitamins, minerals, pros, cons.
- Opener is `FOOD!` then `RANKED!`; final spoken block is always `X tier.`
- Overall score remains display-only and is not spoken.
- Macro sections start with the headline macro amount, then up to two standout visible submacros: one strong point and one weak point when defensible.
- For arrow submacros, stronger green/red arrow bands matter more than raw weighted score when choosing standouts.
- Skip `N/A`, missing, or weakly sourced values.
- Do not repeat protein grams as a protein submacro because it is already in the macro/header.
- Protein visible rows stay collagen, essential amino acids, nonessential amino acids, and bioavailability.
- Vitamins and minerals mention the best defensible DV-backed nutrient and weakest defensible DV-backed nutrient where available.
- The FoodRanked B-vitamin slot is `Vitamin B12`; never use generic `Vitamin B` wording.
- Narration should be short, direct, educational, and snappy. It can have personality, especially for debate foods.
- Do not reintroduce basics already carried by the header: food name, type, kcal, per-100g, image.
- Final outputs have exactly 3 pros and exactly 3 cons.
- Pros/cons should add food-specific context rather than recap macros/vitamins/minerals.
- Good pro/con angles include antioxidants, polyphenols, fermentation, anti-nutrients, absorbability, sourcing, tolerance, digestion, processing burden, satiety, convenience, and meal role.
- Pros/cons should sound like the narrator is reading the on-screen items directly.
- Pros/cons titles should be bite-sized, max 64 characters, and fit the 3-line textbox.
- Spoken audio expands units and abbreviations: `3g` -> `3 grams`, `24mg` -> `24 milligrams`, `DV` -> `daily value`, `8/9` -> `8 out of 9`.
- Subtitles/display keep compact units such as `g`, `mg`, `DV`, and `8/9`.
- Subtitle cues must be max 2 lines and should not split decimals like `37.1g`.

## New Script Narration Rules

- James added a macro narration rule: when a macro section highlights an outstanding submacro, the script should say what that standout is good or bad for, and also explain why that macro section matters for the specific food type.
- This rule was locked into docs and the generator in pushed commit `27ea7d1d` (`Add macro narration context rule`). The generator was verified on bacon, oats, chicken thigh, and the FoodRanked test pack.
- James added a conclusion rule: the summary should collect the food's strengths and weaknesses from across the whole video, then say what the food is good for and why before the final tier reveal.
- Conclusion "good for" uses should come from a structured possible-use list, with examples like energy, hormone health, muscles, bone health, endurance sports, and strength sports. Pick defensible uses based on the actual food data and section evidence.
- The final spoken tier reveal must remain its own last block: `X tier.`
- This conclusion/use-case rule was locked into docs and the generator in pushed commit `c0b890e2` (`Add conclusion use-case summaries`). Generated scripts now include structured `useCases`, `strengthHighlights`, and `weaknessHighlights`.

## Script Standout Selection Updates

- James added a rule that macro and micronutrient sections should try to mention the strongest and weakest marks in the section, for at least two total callouts when defensible, to give those sections more time and substance.
- If the weakest/lowest mark is still decent, phrase it as the "softer support" or "lowest support" mark rather than pretending it is bad.
- For visible protein rows, avoid calling out collagen as the weakest mark for non-meat/protein-forward foods; for oats/grain examples, use bioavailability as the lower protein caveat while keeping essential amino acids as the strongest mark.
- If a vitamin section is low all round, it can be grouped as "vitamins are low all round" while still explaining what vitamins matter for that food type, instead of forcing an awkward best/worst vitamin pair.
- For oats/grain calibration: carbs should highlight glycemic index as the strongest display band and starch as the weakest/softer carb mark; minerals should use magnesium as the strong point and calcium, not potassium, as the low/weak point.
- Do not invent exact glycemic index numbers. If the exact GI is not source-backed, say it is in the strongest display band rather than giving a fake measured value.
