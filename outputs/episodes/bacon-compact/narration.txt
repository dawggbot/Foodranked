Bacon!
-
Ranked!
-
37.1 grams of fat. The good part is 5.8 grams of polyunsaturated fat. The bad part is 12.6 grams of saturated fat. For meat, that saturated fat is the main tradeoff.
-
Carbs are basically irrelevant here. There are no defensible carb submacros to score. For plain cured meat, that is normal, so this section mostly stays out of the way.
-
13.7 grams of protein. Essential amino acids are 9 out of 9, and non-essential amino acids are 10 out of 11. Collagen is only 0.4 grams. For meat, the protein quality is the main upside.
-
Vitamin B12 is the clear mark at 21% daily value. Vitamin D is only 1% daily value. For meat, this is useful B12 support, not a broad vitamin profile.
-
Zinc is the best mineral at 10% daily value. Iron is only 2% daily value, and potassium is 4% daily value. For meat, the mineral section is mostly weak.
-
Pros first: highly palatable and appealing. It is an obvious reason people like it. Works as a strong flavour booster. A small amount can carry a whole plate. Has strong comfort-food appeal. It is instantly recognisable and easy to package in meals.
-
Cons next: sodium is a major drawback. Even a small serving can bring a lot of salt, so it adds up fast. Processing is a major negative. Because bacon is cured meat, the health tradeoff is bigger than the macro numbers alone suggest. Salty, fatty flavour makes portions easy to overdo. That flavour is part of the appeal, but it also makes bacon harder to keep as a small add-on.
-
Best bits are protein quality, vitamin B12, and strong flavour. Weak spots are saturated fat, sodium, processing, and easy portion creep. Best as a small flavour add-on, not an everyday main protein.
-
D tier.
