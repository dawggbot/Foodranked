Bacon!
-
Ranked!
-
37.1 grams of fat. The bright spot is 5.8 grams of polyunsaturated fat, which helps balance the fat profile. The pressure point is 12.6 grams of saturated fat, and that is where bacon starts paying for its flavour. For meats, fat quality decides whether the protein tradeoff feels clean or messy.
-
Carbs are basically out of the picture here. There are no defensible carb submacros to score, and for a plain cured meat, that is normal. This section mostly stays out of the way instead of helping or hurting the ranking.
-
13.7 grams of protein. Essential amino acids land at 9 out of 9, so the protein has a real repair and maintenance role. Non-essential amino acids are also strong at 10 out of 11, while collagen is only 0.4 grams. For meats, protein quality is the main reason bacon still has a serious upside.
-
Vitamin B12 is the clear vitamin mark at 21% daily value, supporting nerves and blood cells. Vitamin D is only 1% daily value, and the rest of the vitamin panel is thin. So bacon is not carrying the broad micronutrient workload you might want from a meat.
-
Zinc is the best mineral mark at 10% daily value, with a modest immune-support angle. Iron is only 2% daily value and potassium is 4% daily value, so the mineral section is mostly weak for a meat entry.
-
Pros first: highly palatable and appealing. That is the obvious reason people like it. Works as a strong flavour booster. A small amount can carry a whole plate. Has strong comfort-food appeal. It is instantly recognisable and easy to package in meals.
-
Cons next: sodium is a major drawback. Even a small serving can bring a lot of salt, so it adds up fast. Processing is a major negative. Because bacon is cured meat, the health tradeoff is bigger than the macro numbers alone suggest. Salty, fatty flavour makes portions easy to overdo. That appeal is real, but it also makes bacon harder to keep as a small add-on.
-
So Bacon still has real protein quality, useful vitamin B12, a little zinc, and a strong flavour role. But saturated fat, low vitamin D, low iron, heavy sodium, processing, and portion creep pull it down hard. It is best as a small flavour tool or occasional comfort-food add-on, not as a core everyday protein.

D tier.
