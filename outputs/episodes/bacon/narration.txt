Bacon ranked.

37.1 grams of fat. Polyunsaturated fat is 5.8 grams, helping with cell structure and healthy signalling. Saturated fat is 12.6 grams, working against a cleaner fat profile, and for meats, fat quality is a major tradeoff. For meats, fat quality matters a lot once the protein is already there.

No defensible carb submacros to call out. In this category, carbs barely matter.

13.7 grams of protein. Essential amino acids score is 9 out of 9, making the protein useful for repair and maintenance. Collagen is only 0.4 grams, weak for connective-tissue protein, so for meats, it is not bringing much connective-tissue protein. Strong protein is expected here, so the rest of the profile decides how high it climbs.

Vitamin B12 is 21% daily value, useful for nerve and blood-cell support. Vitamin D is only 1% daily value, weak for bone and immune support, which is a small miss for meats.

Zinc is 10% daily value, useful for immune support. Iron is only 2% daily value, weak for oxygen transport, which is a weak mark for meats.

Pros first: highly palatable and appealing. A real-world reason people love it. Works as a strong flavour booster. Useful in meals even in smaller amounts. Has strong comfort-food appeal. Popular and instantly legible for content packaging.

The drawbacks next: sodium is a major drawback. A central practical health issue for bacon. Processing is a major negative. A key downside now that more processed foods are in scope. Salty-fatty profile encourages portion creep. The flavour is a strength, but it also makes moderation harder.

Big strengths are protein, palatable and appealing, and strong flavour booster, but the biggest weaknesses are low iron, low vitamin D, and saturated fat. It is good for muscles, blood and oxygen support, and immune support because protein still gives it a muscle-support angle, vitamin B12 supports the blood-and-oxygen side, and zinc gives the immune-support side a reason to exist.

D tier.
