Positives first: fresh EVOO can bring polyphenols. The bitter, peppery quality is part of the real extra-virgin advantage. Mostly monounsaturated fat identity. That gives it a different fat-quality role from many seed oils and animal fats. Helps carry fat-soluble nutrients. A little oil can make vegetable-heavy meals work better.
-
Downsides next: quality drops with heat, light, and age. The best part of extra virgin olive oil depends on freshness and storage. Extremely energy dense. It is still pure fat, so the calories arrive fast. Easy to overpour without noticing. Free-pouring can quietly add a lot more than intended.
-
Big strengths are fat quality and vitamin E, but the biggest weaknesses are quality drops with heat, light, and age and extremely energy dense.
