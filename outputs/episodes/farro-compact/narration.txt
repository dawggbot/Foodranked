-
The upsides first: chewy texture holds up in full meals. It works in soups, salads, and grain bowls without turning to mush. Batch-cooks into a practical meal base. One pot can cover several meals and takes stronger toppings well. Pearled farro is easier to cook. Removing some outer bran shortens the cooking time and makes it more approachable.
-
Downsides next: pearling strips away part of the bran. That convenience costs some fibre and micronutrient potential. Contains gluten. It is not suitable for coeliac disease or a gluten-free diet. Still needs cooking and planning. It is less instant than rice or couscous and definitely is not grab-and-go.
-
Best bits are protein and omega 3. Weak spots are low vitamin E and low calcium. Best for heart health and energy because fibre is useful for heart health and the carb section gives it a clear fuel role.
