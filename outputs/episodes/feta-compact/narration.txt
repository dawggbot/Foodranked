Feta!
-
Ranked!
-
21.5 grams of fat. Omega 3 is 265 milligrams, supporting cell and heart function, but modest beside oily fish. Saturated fat is 13.3 grams, seriously heavy even for cheese. Polyunsaturated fat is only 0.59 grams, with little unsaturated fat to soften the blow. Cholesterol is 89 milligrams, adding more baggage. For dairy, fat quality matters, and feta's is rough.
-
3.9 grams of carbs. Sugar and starch are both 0 grams, and the glycemic index around 27 sits low. Fibre is also 0 grams, so feta brings no plant-based fullness or digestion support. For dairy, this section is harmless rather than helpful. Let the meal bring the fibre.
-
14.2 grams of protein. Essential amino acids score 9 out of 9. The full set makes this useful protein for repair and maintenance. Bioavailability is 92%, meaning most of that dairy protein should be digestible and usable. Nonessential amino acids score 8 out of 11, a softer mark, but your body can make many itself. For dairy, protein quality is a real strength.
-
Vitamin B12 is 70% daily value, the surprise star here. It keeps nerves working properly and helps make red blood cells that carry oxygen around the body. Vitamin A is 14% daily value, a smaller but useful amount for vision and immune support. For dairy, B12 does the heavy lifting and vitamin A is a decent extra, rather than this being a broad vitamin powerhouse.
-
Calcium is 38% daily value, strong for bones, teeth, and normal muscle contraction. Zinc is 26% daily value, supporting immunity and wound healing. Magnesium is only 5% daily value, offering little help with energy production or nerve and muscle function. For dairy, calcium is the expected headline, zinc is a useful bonus, and magnesium is the weak spot.
-
The upsides first: a little feta can change a whole plate. The salty tang means a small crumble can wake up salads, eggs, wraps, and vegetables. Real Protected Designation of Origin feta has to come from Greece and use sheep's milk, sometimes mixed with goat's milk. The crumble format is genuinely useful, spreading flavour across a meal without needing a thick slab of cheese.
-
The drawbacks next: the salt is the real catch. Brining pushes sodium to roughly 1.1 grams per 100 grams, so portions matter even before the saturated fat gets involved. It is still a dairy food. Milk allergy or poor dairy tolerance can rule it out completely. The sharp flavour can take over. Feta works brilliantly as an accent, but it is not always what you want dominating the entire meal.
-
Best bits are complete, highly usable protein, huge vitamin B12, strong calcium and zinc, and loads of flavour from a small amount. Weak spots are the rough saturated-fat profile, very high sodium, no fibre, and barely any magnesium. Best for practical meals where you want a protein-backed flavour boost rather than a slab of cheese. Crumble a little over vegetables, grains, or eggs and let the rest of the meal bring the fibre and volume.
-
A tier.
