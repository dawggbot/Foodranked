Flax Seeds!
-
Ranked!
-
42.2 grams of fat. Omega 3 is an enormous 22813 milligrams, almost all ALA, the plant form your body can use directly and convert in small amounts into longer-chain omega 3s. Saturated fat is only 3.7 grams, keeping the fat tradeoff unusually clean. For seeds, fat quality is one of the main jobs.
-
28.9 grams of carbs. Fibre is 27.3 grams, so nearly the entire carb number is fibre rather than digestible starch or sugar. The glycemic index is 35, and starch is only 1.3 grams. For seeds, that is what you want: fibre doing useful work without turning the food into a big starch hit.
-
18.3 grams of protein. Essential amino acids score is 9 out of 9, so every essential group clears FoodRanked's useful-amount threshold. Nonessential amino acids score 9 out of 11, still strong but the softer mark. Protein digestibility is about 92%, meaning most of that protein can be digested and absorbed, making its amino acids available. Lysine is still the limiting amino acid, so highly digestible does not mean perfectly balanced. For seeds, that makes flax strong support protein rather than a perfect standalone source.
-
Vitamins are low all round. Vitamin K is the best mark at only 4% daily value, while vitamin E is 2%. Vitamin K supports normal blood clotting and bone proteins, and vitamin E helps protect cell membranes. Seeds usually win through fats, fibre, and minerals.
-
Magnesium is 93% daily value, an excellent amount for energy release plus normal nerve and muscle function. Zinc is 39% and iron is 32% daily value, supporting immunity, tissue repair, and oxygen transport. Potassium is the softer mark at 17%. For seeds, broad mineral density helps justify the dense calories.
-
One of the richest everyday lignan sources. Lignans are plant compounds that gut bacteria can turn into enterolignans, giving flax a named-compound story beyond the visible nutrient rows. Grinds into an egg-free baking binder. Mix ground flax with water and it forms a gel that can help bind pancakes, muffins, and other bakes. Cheap and shelf-stable whole. The intact seeds keep well and are easy to add to porridge, yoghurt, or dough without a full recipe.
-
Whole seeds can pass through partly undigested. Human research found much better lignan availability after crushing or milling, so grinding matters if you want more than the fibre. A sudden fibre jump can upset digestion. Large amounts can bring bloating, fullness, or diarrhoea. Build up gradually. Normal servings are much smaller than 100 grams. A spoonful is useful, but it delivers only a fraction of this stat sheet in a real meal.
-
Best bits are the massive ALA omega 3 mark, extreme fibre, useful protein, magnesium, and the lignan story. Weak spots are the thin vitamin panel, limited lysine, lower payoff from swallowing the seeds whole, and digestive tolerance. Best for heart-health-focused meals, digestion, and easy meal upgrades because the fats and fibre are strong and ground flax is simple to use.
