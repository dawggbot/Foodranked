60.1 grams of carbs. Glycemic index is 32 GI, helping with steadier carb behaviour. Fibre is 10.7 grams, helping with digestion and steadier meals, but it is the softer support mark in this section. For legumes, the carbs look much better when fibre and protein are both backing them up.
-
25.8 grams of protein. That amount is useful enough to count. Bioavailability is 72%, helping more of that protein count, but it is the softer support mark in this section. Protein support is a big part of what makes legumes worth it.
-
Vitamins are low overall. For legumes, vitamin support is secondary to fibre, protein, and minerals, so this section is not adding much.
-
Iron is 37% daily value, useful for oxygen transport. Zinc is 23% daily value, weak for immune support, which is a weak mark for legumes.
-
The upsides first: one of the best budget staple foods. It is unusually cheap, filling, and useful in real meals compared with a lot of healthy-food staples. Very filling for the calories. The fibre and protein combo gives it strong practical fullness. Usually affordable and practical. Makes it easy to use as a real staple rather than a niche health food.
-
Cons next: contains some anti-nutrients. Phytates may reduce mineral uptake somewhat. Can be rough on some people digestively. Not everyone handles legumes equally well. Needs some cooking effort. A small practical friction versus more convenient foods.
-
Big strengths are protein, fibre, and omega 3, but the biggest weaknesses are low vitamin E, low zinc, and contains some anti-nutrients. It is good for heart health, digestion, and energy and endurance sports because fibre supports the heart-health side, fibre supports digestion and steadier meals, and the carb section gives usable fuel.

A tier.
