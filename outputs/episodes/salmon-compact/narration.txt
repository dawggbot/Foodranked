Salmon!
-
Ranked!
-
13 grams of fat. Omega 3 is 2200 milligrams, which is a huge strength here. Saturated fat is 3.1 grams, so the fat profile stays pretty clean for a meat. For meats, fat quality is the main tradeoff, and salmon handles that really well.
-
No defensible carb submacros to call out. For meats, carbs barely matter unless something has been added, so this section is basically neutral.
-
20.4 grams of protein. Essential amino acids score is 9 out of 9, and nonessential amino acids score is 10 out of 11, so the protein quality is strong. Collagen is only 0.3 grams, so the structural-protein side is weak. For meats, complete protein matters a lot, and the 94% bioavailability does bring it back up a lot too.
-
Vitamin B12 is 117% daily value, which is excellent for nerve and blood-cell support. Vitamin D is 66% daily value, which is also a serious mark for bone and immune support. Vitamin C is 0, but for meats, B12 and vitamin D matter much more.
-
Minerals are the weak section. Potassium is 8% daily value, which is the best number here but still low. Iron is only 4% daily value, and zinc is only 4% too. For meats, minerals can add value, but salmon is not winning through this section.
-
The upsides first: easy to build a strong meal around. Works as a real centrepiece food. Helps meals feel complete. Very useful when you want protein without needing lots of extras. Strong healthy-food reputation. In this case, the reputation actually matches the numbers.
-
Cons next: can be expensive. Price can make it less practical. Quality varies with sourcing. Wild, farmed, fresh, and frozen versions can shift the real-world result. Needs good storage and handling. It is not as easy as shelf-stable protein.
-
Best bits are omega 3, vitamin B12, vitamin D, and complete protein. Weak spots are the low minerals, and the practical issues around price, sourcing, and storage. Best as a protein-and-fat source for heart health, muscle support, bone support, immune support, and practical high-protein meals.
-
S tier.
