Ranked!

11.5 grams of fat. Omega 3 is 1480 milligrams, supporting a more useful fat profile. Cholesterol is 142 milligrams, and for meats, fat quality is a major tradeoff.

0 grams of carbs. No defensible carb submacros to call out. In this category, carbs barely matter.
-
24.6 grams of protein. Essential amino acids score is 9 out of 9, with 95% bioavailability, making the protein useful for repair and maintenance. Collagen is 1.5 grams, so for meats, it is not bringing much connective-tissue protein.
-
Vitamin B12 is 370% daily value, useful for nerve and blood-cell support. Vitamin D is 24% daily value, a small miss for meats.
-
Iron is 16% daily value, useful for oxygen transport. Potassium is 8% daily value, a weak mark for meats.
-
The upsides first: small-fish nutrient density is elite. Omega-3s, protein, and micronutrients all come together unusually well here. Easy to centre a meal around. Practical meal value matters. Gives a straightforward protein solution. The category often wins on simplicity and reliability.
-
The drawbacks next: cost and storage can be a pain. Practical downsides still count. Fat quality can go sideways fast. Some meats are strong until the fat profile shows up. Quality swings a lot by cut and processing. The category can look very different depending on what version you actually buy.
-
Big strengths are omega 3 and protein, but the biggest weaknesses are cost and storage can be a pain and fat quality can go sideways fast.
