-
30.2 grams of carbs. Glycemic index is 16 GI, helping with steadier carb behaviour. Starch is 3 grams, weak for useful staple-carb payoff, which is a meaningful miss for legumes. For legumes, the carbs look much better when fibre and protein are both backing them up.
-
36.5 grams of protein. That amount is useful enough to count. Bioavailability is 78%, helping more of that protein count, but it is the lowest mark here but still a strong one. Protein support is a big part of what makes legumes worth it.
-
Vitamin C is 10% daily value, useful for collagen formation and antioxidant support. Vitamin E is 6% daily value, weak for vitamin support, which is a weak mark for legumes.
-
Magnesium is 70% daily value, adding useful mineral support. Zinc is 37% daily value, weak for immune support, which is a weak mark for legumes.
-
Pros first: extreme protein/mineral legume. Tests whether legumes can handle a very non-typical high-fat, high-protein member. Very low glycemic behaviour. Strong category fit. Many practical uses. Broad food-system relevance.
-
The drawbacks next: anti-nutrients matter. Preparation and form matter a lot. Often consumed in processed forms. Whole soybeans are not the only real-world form. Can be rough on some people. Practical downside.
-
Big strengths are protein, omega 3, and fibre, but the biggest weaknesses are low vitamin E, low zinc, and anti-nutrients matter. It is good for heart health, digestion, and muscles and strength sports because fibre supports the heart-health side, fibre supports digestion and steadier meals, and the protein section supports repair and maintenance.
