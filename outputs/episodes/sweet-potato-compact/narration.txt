Sweet Potato!
-
Ranked!
-
0.1 grams of fat. Saturated fat is 0 grams, supporting a cleaner fat profile. For tubers, fat is mostly a preparation check, not the main job.
-
20.1 grams of carbs. Glycemic index is 44, helping with steadier carb behaviour. Fibre is 3 grams, with only modest fullness and digestion support, so that's a real downside for tubers. For tubers, carb quality decides a lot.
-
1.6 grams of protein. Protein barely matters here. For tubers, protein is usually limited, so the other sections have to carry more.
-
Vitamin A is 160% daily value, useful for vision and immune support. Vitamin C is only 3% daily value, not bringing much collagen or antioxidant support. For tubers, vitamins can lift a staple carb.
-
Potassium is 10% daily value, useful for fluid balance. Iron is only 3% daily value, not bringing much oxygen-transport support. For tubers, minerals matter most when potassium shows up strongly.
-
The upsides first: beta-carotene is the signature upside. The orange flesh gives sweet potato a clear carotenoid identity. Fuller texture helps real meals. That practical fullness is one reason it can work beyond the numbers. Sweet role is easy to understand. It can be useful when sweetness is the job and the portion is deliberate.
-
The drawbacks next: staple role needs portion context. It works best when the rest of the meal keeps it grounded. Large portions push carbs up fast. It is still a starchy tuber, so the carb load can climb quickly. Needs the right supporting foods. It works better as part of a fuller plate than as the whole answer.
-
Best bits are fibre and vitamin A. Weak spots are low iron and low protein amount. Best for immune support and practical meals because vitamin A helps support the immune system and it's easy to build meals around.
