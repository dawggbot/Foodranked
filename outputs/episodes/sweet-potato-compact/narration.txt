Ranked!

1.6 grams of protein. So protein is barely part of the case.
-
Vitamin A is 160% daily value, useful for vision and immune support. Vitamin C is only 3% daily value, a weak mark for tubers.
-
Potassium is 10% daily value, useful for fluid balance. Iron is only 3% daily value, a weak mark for tubers.
-
The upsides first: beta-carotene is the signature upside. The orange flesh gives sweet potato a clear carotenoid identity. Better fibre support than many tubers. The fibre helps the carb package look more useful. Naturally sweet without added sugar. It brings sweetness without needing a processed dessert format.
-
The drawbacks next: glycemic impact changes with cooking method. Baking, boiling, cooling, and mashing can change the real-world carb behaviour. Large portions push carbs up fast. It is still a starchy tuber, so the carb load can climb quickly. Protein is barely part of the case. The tuber job is energy and carotenoids, not meaningful protein.
-
Big strengths are fibre and vitamin A, but the biggest weaknesses are glycemic impact changes with cooking method and large portions push carbs up fast.
