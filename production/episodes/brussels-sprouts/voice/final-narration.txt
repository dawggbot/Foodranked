Brussels Sprouts!
-
Ranked!
-
0.6 grams of fat. Cholesterol is 0 milligrams, keeping the fat tradeoff clean. Monounsaturated fat is only 0.02 grams, so there is barely any useful fat here. For vegetables, fat is not really the job anyway.
-
9.6 grams of carbs. Glycemic index is 15, landing in the strongest band for steadier carb behaviour. Fibre is 4.8 grams, strong for a vegetable and useful for digestion and fullness. Starch is only 0.6 grams, but that is expected for a non-starchy vegetable.
-
4 grams of protein. Protein digestibility is 65%, which is decent for a whole vegetable. Essential amino acids score is 0 out of 9 because the total amount is too small to clear any useful-amount threshold. Protein helps a little here, but it is not why you eat Brussels sprouts.
-
Vitamin C is 159% daily value, excellent for immune support and collagen production. Vitamin K is 148% daily value, excellent for normal blood clotting and bone support. Vitamin D and vitamin B12 are both at zero, but that is expected for a vegetable.
-
Potassium is 10% daily value, giving the best mineral mark and helping with fluid balance. Calcium is only 3% daily value, weak for bone support. Minerals are easily the softest section here.
-
Pros first: glucosinolate-rich brassica profile. That gives Brussels sprouts a food-specific plant-compound story beyond the visible vitamin bars. Lots of whole-food volume without much calorie pressure. Useful when you want a bigger plate without making the meal heavy. Flexible side dish when roasted, steamed, or shredded. That makes it practical across a lot of normal meals.
-
Downsides next: bitter flavour is polarising. The same glucosinolate family behind part of the plant-compound story also contributes to the taste. Can cause gas and bloating for some people. Raffinose and fermentable fibre are not equally comfortable for every gut. Boiling can wash out part of the payoff. It causes more nutrient loss than gentler methods such as steaming.
-
Best bits are fibre, low glycemic impact, vitamins C and K, and the glucosinolate profile. Weak spots are useful fat, useful amino-acid amounts, minerals, and digestive tolerance. Best for digestion, immune support, bone support, and low-calorie volume because the fibre helps the gut, the vitamins do real work, and it adds a lot to a plate without much energy.
-
A tier.
