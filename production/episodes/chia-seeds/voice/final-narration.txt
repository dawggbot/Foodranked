Magnesium is 80% daily value, the clear mineral strength. Calcium is 49% daily value, iron is 43% daily value, and zinc is 42% daily value, so the backup mineral profile is broad too. For seeds, this is a real strength.

Pros first: fuller texture helps real meals. The gel can make yogurt, oats, and smoothies feel more filling. ALA omega-3 is the headline fat. That is the food-specific reason chia stands out among seeds. Easy to boost meals invisibly. A small spoonful can disappear into everyday meals.
-
Cons next: works best with enough fluid. Dry chia is awkward unless it is soaked or mixed properly. Phytates add an absorption caveat. The mineral numbers are high, but absorption is not automatic. Gel texture and digestion can be divisive. The same thing that helps satiety can put some people off.
-
Best bits are omega 3, fibre, and minerals. Weak spots are vitamins, gated protein quality, and texture tolerance. Best for heart health, digestion, and meal-boosting because the fibre is huge, the fat profile is unusual, and a small amount can upgrade simple foods.
