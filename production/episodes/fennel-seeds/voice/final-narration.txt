Fennel Seeds!
-
Ranked!
-
15 grams of fat. Saturated fat is 0.5 grams, which is nice and low. Omega 3 is 250 milligrams, helping a bit, but not enough to be amazing for seeds. Polyunsaturated fat is 6 grams, which sounds solid, but for seeds it is still not doing loads. There is 9.9 grams of monounsaturated fats though, which brings the score back up a bit.
-
52 grams of carbs. Fibre is 40 grams, which is massive and easily the best part here. Glycemic index is 15, helping with steadier carb behaviour. Starch is 10 grams, adding some usable carb energy, but fibre is clearly doing the heavy lifting. For seeds, carbs matter most when fibre is this strong.
-
16 grams of protein. That is decent on paper, but it still is not really carrying the food. Essential amino acids score is 0 out of 9, not giving much repair-and-maintenance protein support. For seeds, protein helps, but it needs better quality to become the main reason to use them.
-
Vitamins are not really showing up here. Vitamin E is 7% daily value, which is too low to score. For seeds, vitamin E matters most, and fennel seeds are not bringing enough of it.
-
Calcium is 120% daily value, which is huge for bone support. Iron is 100% daily value, adding real oxygen-transport support. Magnesium is 96% daily value, also helping with muscle and nerve support. For seeds, minerals are the clearest reason this works on the stat sheet.
-
The upsides first: tiny food with real nutrient density. The numbers are genuinely serious for something this small. Easy to add into meals. A small amount can fit into cooking without changing the whole plate. Richer texture can help satisfaction. That can make a sprinkle feel more useful than plain seasoning.
-
The drawbacks next: spice-like portions cap the real payoff. The numbers are huge, but the real serving is tiny. Strong flavour can take over a dish. That anise-like taste is useful, but it is not exactly neutral. Whole seeds can be awkward to eat. Texture and chewing matter, especially when they are sprinkled in rather than ground.
-
Best bits are fibre, calcium, iron, and magnesium. Weak spots are low vitamin E, limited protein quality, and the fact that normal portions are tiny. Best as a small mineral-and-fibre boost in cooking, not as a main food. Basically, the score sheet looks strong, but real-life serving sizes pull it back down.
