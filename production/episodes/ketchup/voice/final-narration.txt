Ketchup!
-
Ranked!
-
0.1 grams of fat. Cholesterol is 0 milligrams and saturated fat is only 0.01 grams, so there is almost no saturated-fat baggage. Polyunsaturated fat is just 0.04 grams and omega 3 is only 1 milligram, so there is no useful fat story either. In the misc category, being nearly fat-free does not rescue the rest of the food.
-
27.4 grams of carbs. Sugar is 21.3 grams, while fibre is only 0.3 grams, so very little slows the carbohydrate down. The glycemic index is 55, although a normal spoonful keeps the real glycemic load smaller than the 100-gram sheet suggests. For a sweet condiment, this is still a weak carb profile.
-
1 gram of protein. Essential amino acids score 0 out of 9 because none reaches a useful amount at this food quantity. Protein digestibility is 78%, but that percentage only describes how digestible the tiny amount is. It does not turn ketchup into a protein food. In this category, the protein section barely matters.
-
Vitamin E is the best mark at 10% daily value and vitamin C is 5%. Vitamin E helps protect cell membranes from oxidative damage, but the rest of the panel is tiny or absent. For a processed tomato condiment, there is a little vitamin value, just nowhere near enough to carry the score.
-
Potassium is the best mineral at only 6% daily value, while calcium is just 1%. Potassium supports fluid balance and normal muscle function, but this amount is small. For the misc category, useful minerals can create a reason to exist, and ketchup is not getting one here.
-
The upsides first: processed tomato brings a lycopene angle. The USDA record contains about 12 milligrams of lycopene per 100 grams, and processing tomato can make that carotenoid easier to absorb. Strong flavour can replace richer sauces. A little sweetness, acidity, and tomato umami can make a meal more appealing without needing a heavy creamy sauce. Cheap, familiar, and easy to use. It is widely available, needs no preparation, and works as a quick condiment for plenty of everyday foods.
-
The drawbacks next: salt stacks up quickly. The USDA record contains 907 milligrams of sodium per 100 grams, which is a real tradeoff hidden outside the visible mineral rows. Acidity can bother reflux. Tomato and vinegar make this a rough fit for some people whose reflux is triggered by acidic foods. Squeeze bottles encourage portion creep. A freehand squirt is easy to underestimate, especially when the sauce is spread across chips, burgers, or several parts of a meal.
-
Best bits are the lycopene angle, strong flavour, convenience, and some value as a lighter swap for creamy sauces. Weak spots are the sugar-heavy carb profile, almost no fibre or protein, weak vitamins and minerals, plus the sodium and reflux tradeoffs. Best for occasional low-calorie flavour rather than nutrition. Essentially, ketchup takes tomatoes, concentrates the flavour, then adds enough baggage that the useful tomato story cannot save the score.
