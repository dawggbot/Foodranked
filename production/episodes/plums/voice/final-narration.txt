Plums!
-
Ranked!
-
0.3 grams of fat. Saturated fat is 0.017 grams, supporting a cleaner fat profile. Although the fat profile isn't that important for fruits.
-
11.4 grams of carbs. Glycemic index is low at 39, meaning carbs release slower and steadier, this helps avoid sharp blood sugar spikes. Fibre is 1.4 grams, which provides only a modest fullness and digestion support, this matters as fiber is an important factor for fruits. Sugar control and fibre are the most important bits of the carbohydrate profile of fruits.
-
0.7 grams of protein. Not enough protein to be a real strength. For fruits, protein isn't really something to look out for.
-
Vitamin C is 11% daily value, useful for collagen formation and antioxidant support. Vitamin A is only 2% daily value, not bringing much vision or immune support. For fruit, vitamin C and vitamin A matter most.
-
Potassium is 3% daily value, which may be the best number here, but still too low to matter much. All other minerals aren't worth talking about. For fruit, minerals are mostly just an extra bonus, and only matter when they are actually worth talking about.
-
Pros first: easy fresh-fruit snack. It works with no peeling or cooking, so it is easy to use when the fruit is ripe. Peel adds tart colour. Keeping the skin gives more plum colour, texture, and sharp-sweet contrast. Works fresh or cooked. It can go into yoghurt, oats, salads, compote, sauces, or desserts without much effort.
-
Cons next: bruises easily. Soft fruit bruises quickly, so quality can drop fast after transport or storage. Ripeness window is short. The best eating window is narrow, and underripe or overripe plums are much less useful. Stone interrupts snacking. The pit makes them less convenient than fully edible bite-size fruit like berries or grapes.
-
Best bits are that it's an easy fresh-fruit snack and low glycemic index. Weak spots are low fiber and low vitamin A. Best for quick on the go snacks, low-calorie to volume ratio, and its ability to compliment many other foods.

D tier.
