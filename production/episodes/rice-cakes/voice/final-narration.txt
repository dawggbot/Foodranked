Rice Cakes!
-
Ranked!
-
3 grams of fat. Saturated fat is only 0.6 grams, so the fat profile is pretty clean. Omega 3 is just 20 milligrams, though, so there is basically no seed-style fat upside here. For grains, fat is generally all around low anyway.
-
81.5 grams of carbs. Starch is 77 grams, so rice cakes do bring quick staple-carb energy. But the glycemic index is 82, which is rough for steadier carb behaviour, and 4.3 grams of fibre is not enough to rescue it. Carb quality matters most for grains.
-
7 grams of protein. The essential amino acids score is 0 out of 9, and the nonessential amino acids score is 0 out of 11, so this is not doing much as a protein food, but this is to be expected. For grains, protein can help, but rice cakes are mostly just puffed carb.
-
Vitamins are low overall. Vitamin E is only 1% daily value, and there is no meaningful vitamin backup here. For grains, that is not automatically fatal, but rice cakes do not make up for it elsewhere.
-
Magnesium is 13% daily value, which is the best mineral mark, but still a little low. Calcium is only 2% daily value, and iron and zinc stay low too. For grains, minerals can help round out the score, but this section is weak.
-
Positives first: portable and convenient. Very easy to use as a quick snack base. Easy to portion and carry. Thanks to the simple packaging advantage. Works with many toppings. Practical flexibility for snack content.
-
Downsides next: does not justify taking up your grain slot. Other grain foods usually bring much more actual nutritional value. Usually needs help to feel worthwhile. It often relies on toppings or pairings to become a decent snack. Not very filling for the calories. Can be unsatisfying unless built up with other foods.
-
Best bits are convenience, portability, and easy topping flexibility. Weak spots are the high glycemic index, low protein quality, weak vitamins, and weak minerals. Best as a light snack base or texture food, not as a strong staple by itself.

D tier.
